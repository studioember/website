## Goal, buyer, and qualification

**Business goal:** win scoped, paid planning engagements that are profitable to deliver. **Initial advertising goal:** generate relevant discovery bookings that become attended, qualified conversations.

Start with engineering leaders, technical founders, and platform/development teams facing an actual workload, delivery, or operating-model decision. The person contacting us should own the problem or be able to involve its owner. This is a buyer hypothesis, not a claim that job titles can be precisely targeted in Search.

A qualified conversation has a real organizational problem, a plausible fit with assessment or planning, a path to the decision-maker, and willingness to discuss paid work and a realistic timeline. A booked call alone does not meet this definition. Students, job applicants, vendors selling to us, and requests for free implementation are not qualified opportunities. A prospect who should use a simpler platform can still be an excellent planning client.

**Promise:** understand the options and leave paid planning with usable decisions and next steps. **Immediate CTA:** book a discovery call with Nathan. Do not describe the discovery call as a free audit, promise savings, publish provisional service prices, or imply that planning obligates implementation.

## Budget, bidding, and economics

**Proposed pilot:** $600 in media over 28 days, entirely on Search. This is an optional learning budget, not a market benchmark, spending authorization, or minimum needed to succeed. $600 divided by 28 is about $21.43 per day for planning only.

Prefer a **campaign total budget** with explicit start/end dates if the account offers it with the intended bidding setup. Google currently documents total budgets for Search; a total-budget campaign is billed up to its total, but has no daily spending cap and may spend unevenly. Verify the combination and saved ceiling before enabling it. Do not silently change bidding merely to fit a budget option. [Campaign total budgets](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15137812?hl=en)

If that combination is unavailable, revise the setup before activation. An average daily budget is not a fixed daily cap or a guaranteed 28-day total. For most campaigns Google can charge up to twice the average daily budget in one day and applies monthly limits. Alerts and pause rules help but are not hard ceilings because reporting and execution can lag. Do not label `$21.43/day` a guaranteed `$600` cap. [Daily budget overdelivery](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1704443?hl=en)

**Initial bidding recommendation:** Maximize Clicks with a researched maximum CPC bid limit, no positive bid adjustments, and the narrow keyword/network settings above. This is a controlled traffic/relevance experiment; Maximize Clicks optimizes clicks, not qualified leads. Manual CPC is an alternative if hands-on bid control is preferred. No numerical CPC cap is prescribed without keyword estimates and an acceptable acquisition cost. [Maximize Clicks controls](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268626?hl=en)

Once real booking attribution is reliable and enough representative conversions accumulate to evaluate lead quality, consider a separate Maximize Conversions test. There is no universal conversion-count threshold in this plan. Do not set a guessed target CPA, optimize to button clicks, or swap strategies repeatedly during the pilot.

### Hand-check the economics

Use contribution after delivery costs rather than headline project revenue:

- Allowable acquisition cost per client = expected contribution × chosen acquisition allocation.
- Allowable cost per qualified call = allowable acquisition cost per client × qualified-call-to-client rate.
- Allowable CPC = allowable cost per qualified call × click-to-qualified-call rate.

**Illustration only:** $4,000 contribution × 25% acquisition allocation = $1,000 allowable acquisition cost. If 20% of qualified calls become clients, the allowance is $200 per qualified call. If 5% of clicks become qualified calls, the implied CPC allowance is $10. These are invented inputs to demonstrate the calculation, not forecasts, prices, or Studio Ember conversion rates. Replace every input with Nathan's economics and observed results; include other acquisition labor/costs when evaluating full profitability.

At $600, hypothetical average CPCs of $5 / $15 / $30 buy 120 / 40 / 20 clicks respectively. None of these imply a lead count. Small samples can be inconclusive even after the full budget is spent.

## Conversion measurement: launch gate

Follow [the measurement guide](measurement.md). Existing code recognizes `page_type=kubernetes_consulting`, sets `service_path=planning`, and distinguishes booking clicks from confirmed embedded bookings. This is instrumentation, not proof that Google Ads can attribute a booking to a paid click.

1. Link the correct GA4 property and Google Ads account; verify the property uses `G-NYVTLFQRSZ`. Enable auto-tagging and check its behavior through the actual HTTPS host and redirects.
2. Mark `generate_lead` as a GA4 key event and create/import its Google Ads conversion action. Inspect the action in Ads: make it Primary for this campaign's booking goal and use **One** counting for leads. Exclude unrelated account-default goals. Keep consultation clicks, email clicks, topic views, and video views out of primary conversions. Do not also count a second tag for the same booking. [GA4 conversion import](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10632359?hl=en-AU)
3. **Resolve the click-attribution uncertainty:** `assets/js/analytics.js` sets a query-free `page_location`, accepts only safe UTM slugs, and forwards those slugs to Cal. It does not explicitly preserve Google Ads click IDs in the lead record, and `booking.js` disables arbitrary query forwarding. UTM reporting alone does not prove auto-tagging, cross-domain attribution, or offline conversion matching. Google's recommended Ads integration uses auto-tagging; inspect Tag Assistant and actual collected attribution before assuming compatibility. Do not remove the site's data minimization or forward arbitrary parameters as a shortcut. Any required tracking code change is separate work. [Google Ads URL tagging](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10723132?hl=en)
4. Verify the production CTA opens the correct event and that bookings deliver confirmation and calendar notifications. Use the existing local harness for callback logic. Check an authorized real booking when available; do not create a fake customer or click a live ad repeatedly just for QA. Record the source evidence and conversion action receiving it, allowing for reporting latency.
5. Reconcile Cal records with embedded `generate_lead` events. Bookings completed directly on cal.com are outside current site completion tracking; email clicks do not prove an email was sent. Keep these outcomes in the private lead log and label unmatched attribution. If material conversions cannot be attributed, fix measurement before spend or explicitly redesign the experiment around that limitation.
6. Publish an accurate privacy notice and decide consent handling for the actual audience and tracking setup. No remarketing pixel, customer-list upload, or enhanced-conversions integration is authorized or installed by this plan.

Do not launch conversion bidding until the conversion action and paid-click attribution are verified. The ad account settings, billing/advertiser verification, actual delivery, and account-side diagnostics have not been inspected in this work.

### Pause, diagnose, and continue rules

These are proposed management rules, not statistical benchmarks:

- **Pause immediately:** broken destination or booking flow, unexpected billing/settings change, misleading ad copy, materially wrong geography, or loss of measurement needed to evaluate the experiment.
- **At $150 spent:** review actual query intent, CPC, and tracking. Obvious irrelevant queries get exclusions immediately. Do not require a booking by this spend level.
- **At $300 spent:** review all inquiries and the click-to-booking path. If there are no qualified conversations, decide whether traffic is irrelevant, offer/proof is weak, booking has friction, or the sample is simply too small. A revision needs an explicit hypothesis; continuing is a conscious use of the remaining research budget.
- **At $600 or day 28:** stop this pilot and reconcile results. No automatic rollover or budget increase. With too little relevant traffic, report “inconclusive at this budget,” not “Google does not work.”
- **Continue only with evidence:** repeatable relevant inquiries, acceptable fit, and economics that make sense under realistic close-rate assumptions. One promising conversation can justify investigation but does not establish a stable acquisition cost.
- **Scale cautiously:** fund a second defined experiment before a broad rollout. Change budget, targeting, creative, or bidding separately where practical so results can be interpreted.
